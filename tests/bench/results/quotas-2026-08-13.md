## Verdict

**The quota path is cheap, and the documented overshoot is real but small.**

- **Per-call tax ~0.17 ms total** (Part 1: increment 0.091 ms, total_since 0.082 ms p50) —
  under 1% of a ~3 ms bridged call. The quota feature (one read + one write per forwarded
  tools/call) is effectively free.
- **The row-lock is NOT the bottleneck** (Part 2): hot-bucket and distinct-bucket curves are
  nearly identical (~21k inc/s at 128-way, p50 ~5.5 ms). The atomic upsert holds the row only
  for the UPDATE itself, so contention is negligible — the ceiling is the writer pool and
  round-trips, same as any other DB write. The issue's "find the row-lock queue point"
  hypothesis is answered: there isn't a meaningful one.
- **`total_since` does not degrade with history** (Part 3): 0.083 → 0.085 ms p50 from 24 to
  720 hour buckets. The keyed index keeps a month-period check flat; the "probably fine by
  inspection" guess is now measured.
- **Overshoot is bounded and typically ~1.5-2.5× the quota** (Part 4): quota_calls=5,
  allowed 7-15 (typical ~11) at a 20-way burst, 5-13 (typical ~8) at 50-200-way bursts. The
  race never approaches the burst size — the event loop's serialization keeps the check-then-
  act window narrow, so the overshoot stays ~2-3× quota regardless of burst size past ~50.
  This is the measured number behind docs/quotas.md's "bounded by the burst size" claim, and
  it matches the accepted-limitation framing: soft budget, small bounded overshoot, no
  re-litigation.
