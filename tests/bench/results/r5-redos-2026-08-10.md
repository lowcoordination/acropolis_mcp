# R5 measurement results — ReDoS-mitigation forkserver cost (issue #32)

Measured 2026-08-10 with `python -m tests.bench.bench_redos` (harness committed alongside
this file). Machine: devbox (Python 3.14.6, venv, no load). Reproduce with:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m tests.bench.bench_redos
```

## Part 1 — added per-match cost of one `block_pattern` (100 iters, warmed)

| args | bytes | no-rules p50/p99 | block-pattern p50/p99 | added p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| small (`query` present) | 36 | 0.001/0.033 ms | **108.6/145.3 ms** | **~109 ms** |
| medium (no `query` param) | 1027 | 0.001/0.002 ms | 0.001/0.002 ms | ~0 |
| large (no `query` param) | 20415 | 0.001/0.002 ms | 0.001/0.002 ms | ~0 |

Key finding: the cost is **zero unless the call carries the rule's named parameter**. The rule
(`param_rules[tool][param]`) only fires when that param is present; calls that don't carry it
never touch the forkserver. So the ~109 ms figure applies only to calls that include the
guarded argument.

The ~109 ms is **~5× the ~22 ms documented in `argus/policy.py`** (measured on this
machine/Python 3.14; the 22 ms was the original author's environment). The comment is updated
to note the measurement and machine-dependence.

## Part 2 — concurrency sweep (30 matches/evaluator, query-carrying args)

| concurrency | wall s | matches/s | p50 ms | p99 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3.20 | 9.4 | 104.2 | 136.2 |
| 8 | 3.63 | 66.1 | 106.5 | 185.4 |
| 16 | 3.81 | 126.1 | 124.1 | 161.5 |
| 24 | 5.75 | 125.3 | 189.1 | 234.0 |
| 32 | 7.66 | 125.3 | 252.1 | 279.2 |
| 64 | 15.17 | 126.5 | 503.5 | 539.7 |

**Throughput ceiling ≈ 126 matches/s**, reached at the 16-way `_regex_semaphore` cap and flat
beyond it; tail latency then grows ~linearly with queueing (p50 124 ms → 503 ms from 16 → 64
concurrent). This is exactly the "tail latency degrades before throughput does" profile the
epic's R5 problem statement predicted.

## Part 3 — adversarial worst case (`(a+)+$` vs crafted input, 0.5 s budget)

| concurrency | wall s | p50 ms | p99 ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 1.02 | 505.9 | 511.6 |
| 24 | 1.62 | 569.0 | 1049.5 |

Every such match costs the **full 0.5 s timeout** (process terminated, `UNDETERMINED` →
fail-closed block). At 24 concurrent, 8 queue behind the 16 occupied slots → p99 ~1.05 s. A
single malicious pattern+input pair against a server carrying that `block_pattern` saturates
all 16 slots with 0.5 s matches.

## Go/no-go on an re2 rewrite

**No-go for now — measure-first verdict.** The cost is real but narrowly scoped:

- It binds only when (a) a server configures `block_patterns` at all, and (b) the call carries
  the rule's named parameter. Adoption of `block_patterns` across real deployments is unknown
  and likely low (built-in DLP detectors and allow/deny lists cover most policies).
- The ceiling (~126 matches/s) only binds under sustained concurrent matching on
  rule-configured servers — a homelab-scale gateway with a handful of such rules will not hit
  it.
- A rewrite must preserve fail-closed `UNDETERMINED` semantics and clear the GIL finding bar
  (threads cannot time out a hung `re` match — recorded in `argus/policy.py:22-31`), and re2
  does not support backreferences/lookaround, so the process-based path must remain as
  fallback for rejected patterns — a larger change than the latency it saves at current
  adoption.

Revisit if/when: real deployments with `block_patterns` at scale, or a policy use-case that
puts the guarded param on every call. If revisited, prototype re2 behind `_match_with_timeout`
keeping the process path as fallback, and re-verify fail-closed posture empirically — the
measurement, not the assumption, is the gate.
